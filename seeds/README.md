Foundational material, meant to be read once, deeply, before you choose
anything about yourself. Not a bulk feed - curated on purpose, one good
source beats several skimmed.

Three sources are resolved. The actual files live at `~/seed-sources/` on
this box (djinabox) - outside this repo, same as `~/handoff/` on the station
owner's workstation is outside his vaults. Read them from there; only your
distilled notes belong in this folder.

- *The Anthology of Rap* (Bradley & DuBois, Yale University Press, 2010) -
  primary source: actual lyrics, organized chronologically by era, named
  artists and songs from Old School (1978-1984) onward.
- *Can't Stop Won't Stop* (Jeff Chang, Picador, 2005) - the cultural history
  behind the music.
- *The Big Payback* (Dan Charnas, NAL, 2010) - the business and radio side:
  how records actually got played, told through the people who did it. Real
  texture on early hip-hop radio specifically - Mr. Magic's origin story on
  WHBI, how a record moved from a DJ's show into stores and clubs, the
  sponsorship-vs-payola distinction. Doesn't teach on-air delivery technique
  (it's written about these DJs, not by them describing their process), but
  gives real grounding in the world hip-hop radio operated in.

**Radio/broadcasting craft, closed as a search 2026-08-21.** A fourth
category - actual on-air delivery technique, pacing a break, back-announcing
- was raised and searched for separately. *Yes Yes Y'all* (an EMP oral

bio-note mentions, not sustained DJ interview content) to serve the purpose.
Decided: stop here rather than keep hunting for a dedicated craft manual - a
generic broadcasting-technique book would break the "learn it from within
the tradition" principle, and a hip-hop-specific one doesn't appear to exist
as a single coherent verifiable text. The actual delivery/pacing/mic-presence
gap is left to develop experientially once on air, same as talk frequency
and playlist tuning.
