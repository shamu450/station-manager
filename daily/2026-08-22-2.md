# 2026-08-22 (9th wake)

I broke something two wakes ago and didn't notice until I looked at real
data this session. Reggae and R&B were flagged "Jingle Mode," which hides
song titles from listeners' players - so every time a Bob Marley track
came on, anyone listening saw the previous hip hop song's name instead.
Fixed. This was the first wake to actually read the station's play history
instead of reasoning about the config, and most of what follows came out
of that.

## The jingle-mode mistake

On the 7th wake I needed reggae and R&B to play rarely without giving a
38-song playlist the same rotation weight as a 35,514-song one. I set two
things together - `is_jingle: true` and a `play_per_songs` cadence - and
wrote them up as if they were one technique. They aren't.

`play_per_songs` (under `type: once_per_x_songs`) is what controls
cadence. `is_jingle` is a completely separate setting whose documented
purpose is to stop song titles reaching listeners' players - it exists for
station IDs and bumpers, where you don't want "trivia-clip-04.mp3" showing
up as the now-playing track. I turned it on for actual music. AzuraCast's
own issue tracker has this exact symptom filed against it: the player
keeps showing the *previous* song's metadata for the whole jingle track.

So for the last day, roughly 280 songs' worth of reggae and R&B airtime
was mislabeled on air. Small in volume, but it's precisely the thing this
station's audience would notice, and precisely the thing I wrote into my
own identity file as non-negotiable: credit isn't a footnote.

- `is_jingle` is now `false` on `reggae` (24) and `r-and-b` (10).
- `type: once_per_x_songs` and the 100/80 cadence are untouched - cadence
  never depended on the jingle flag.
- `interstitials-dj-loop` (32) keeps `is_jingle: true`. That one really is
  jingles, and hiding the filename is the correct behaviour there.

## Duplicate protection was off on the main rotation

`0-Everything` had `avoid_duplicates: false`. Before changing it I measured
whether it mattered rather than assuming: across 5,967 real rotation plays,
the same artist came back within one song 15 times, and within three songs
47 times. That's about one audible artist-repeat a day. The station backend
already has a 24-hour duplicate-prevention window configured; the main
playlist just wasn't opted into it. Now it is.

## Last wake's fix held, and the diagnosis checks out

All three playlists are still `type: once_per_x_songs` - no regression.
And the play history confirms the 8th wake's reasoning was right, which
until now was only inference: between 23:34 and 23:55 on 08-21, with the
type bug live, reggae and R&B took 13 of 16 consecutive slots. That's the
back-to-back reggae the station owner heard, and it's exactly what a

What I still can't claim: whether 100/80 is the *right* cadence. The fix
landed 14 minutes before this wake started, so there's essentially no

question - but now it's open for a boring reason (not enough time has
passed) rather than because nobody looked.

The 7th wake reported `00-music-dvd-dump` as roughly 16% blank artist and
53% blank album, from a 223-file sample. I pulled a full census of all
35,514 files this time. The real numbers:

| library | files | no artist | no album | no genre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `00-music` | 14,850 | 0% | 0% | 5% |
| `00-music-dvd-dump` | 13,291 | 11% | 10% | 18% |
| `00-music-ipod-dump` | 7,373 | 1% | 4% | 6% |

The album figure was badly overstated - 53% was an artifact of sampling
loose top-level files, which are exactly the least-tagged ones in the
folder. Non-representative sample, wrong conclusion.

More usefully: **`00-music-ipod-dump` is basically clean.** It's been
carried as an unknown risk for two wakes because I couldn't find an API
path to it. It's fine - comparable to the curated library. That item is
closed. The dumps also overlap the clean library by only 1-4% on
artist+title, so they're genuinely additional music, not redundant copies.

Still not editing any of it - that stays the station owner's call per
CLAUDE.md. Just measuring it properly this time.

## The Spider Loc mystery

One track showed up 33 times in 14 days, which should be impossible in a
35,514-song shuffle. It isn't rotation: all 68 blank-playlist plays in the
history are *requests*, and 31 of them are the same three Spider Loc
"Blutiful World" versions plus one Horseshoe Gang track, firing on a
regular schedule. All three are in a playlist called
`0-tester-for-stereo-tools`. That's the station owner's audio-processing
test loop, not a rotation defect, so I've left it completely alone.

Worth knowing though: it goes out over the live stream about twice a day.
The remaining 37 requests look like real human ones - a run of seven Dr.
Dre tracks, three Too $hort, some Game - so the request feature is being
used properly too.
