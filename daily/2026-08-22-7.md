## The diagnosis I threw away

Before removing anything I wanted to confirm the files were actually bad,
partly so I could tell him *what* was wrong with each one. There's no ffmpeg
on this box, but the station's waveform endpoint returns peak data and MP3
frames parse in plain Python, so both are reachable.

First attempt produced a peak amplitude of exactly 0.008 on all eight files I
checked. Eight identical readings is a bug, not eight identical files — the
waveform `data` array is already normalized to ±1.0, and I'd been dividing it
by 128 because the payload also says `"bits": 8`. That field describes the
source resolution, not the units.

Corrected, the numbers looked damning: 30.6% of "Brothers Keeper" pinned at
full scale, 25.2% of the Three 6 Mafia remix, and that one ending at 0.965
amplitude — sliced off mid-waveform.

Then I checked what normal looks like here, which I should have done first.
A random sample of 119 rotation tracks:

| | p50 | p75 | p90 | p95 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| % of track at full scale | **6.3** | 15.7 | 28.4 | 43.4 | 79.5 |
| amplitude in final second | 0.003 | 0.22 | 0.50 | 0.64 | 0.87 |

**Half this library clips.** 52% of tracks exceed 5% clipping, 18% exceed
20%. And 29% of tracks end at high amplitude, so "stops dead at full volume"
is just how a lot of hip hop ends — not evidence of truncation.

Against that distribution, three of the five flagged files are **cleaner than
the library median**. Two are high but sit inside the top decile of an
ordinary loud collection, not outside it.

So the honest conclusion is that whatever makes a rip bad to his ear — a DJ
drop over the track, a mislabel, a skip, a lo-fi source — doesn't show up in
level statistics, and a bad-rip detector built on clipping would flag half
the station while still missing the real defects. That's written into
`process/azuracast-api.md` so a future wake doesn't build one. The removals
stand on the membership fact, which was never in doubt; the audio analysis
just doesn't support the extra story I wanted to tell about them.

  `00-music` + `00-music-dvd-dump` + `00-music-ipod-dump`, with **zero

owner, `MEMORY.md` and `process/` are for me, and I'd been filing durable
findings in the one that nobody reads at boot.

## The station dropped out twice during this wake

I checked `nowplaying` at the end of the session and got `is_online: false`.
It had recovered by 18:29 and is playing normally now, with a listener. But
two gaps opened up while I was working, and the timing is close enough to my
own writes that I'm not going to explain it away:

```
18:09:23Z  last track ends — playback gapless for the 40 minutes before this
18:10:31Z  I remove 5 files from 0-Everything (5 sequential PUTs, ~22s)
           ... 11.8 minutes of silence ...
18:19:20Z  I upload clip 10 and add it to playlist 32
18:21:08Z  one track plays
18:25:27Z  it ends
           ... 3.6 minutes of silence ...
18:29:02Z  playback resumes on its own, still going
```

The first gap starts 68 seconds after I began editing a 35,499-entry
playlist five times in a row, and there's a plausible mechanism: each edit
makes AzuraCast regenerate the playlist for Liquidsoap, and that playlist is
large.

**What argues against it being me:** gaps like this predate the project.
Across 14.6 days and 6,334 plays there are 8 gaps of two minutes or more,
and the largest on record — **13.4 minutes on 2026-08-12** — is bigger than
today's and happened before any of this work existed. Days with more than
one gap have also happened before (three on 08-20). Both `backendRunning`
and `frontendRunning` stayed true throughout, and the station recovered
without intervention.

So: correlated in time, plausible mechanism, not proven, and the station
does this occasionally on its own. I'd rather log it as unresolved than
argue myself out of it. For future wakes I'll keep bulk edits against
`0-Everything` to a minimum and check `nowplaying` immediately after writing
to it rather than at the end of the session — if the gap reproduces next
time I touch that playlist, that's the answer.
