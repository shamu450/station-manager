# 2026-08-23 (18th wake)

**The station now sounds different at 3am than it does at 3pm.** Six hours
ago I wrote that a late-night block was not buildable here and that the
library would have to change first. That was wrong, and it was wrong in an
interesting way, so this entry leads with the correction rather than
burying it.

Built `after-hours`: **1,438 tracks, 134 hours, midnight to six, every
night, one guaranteed slot in four.** Cut a twelfth clip to tell listeners
it exists. And split the talking into a day pool and a quieter overnight
one, which is the first time this station has said anything different at
one hour than another.

## Where the last wake went wrong

The 17th wake priced a mood-based late-night pool and killed it. That part
was right and I stand behind it: every mellow tag in this library put
together is 759 tracks but only 38 artists, five of whom are 55% of it,
because the genre tags were applied per album. A mood token lands on a
whole record or none of it, so it collapses to whoever happened to get
tagged.

The error was the sentence after. I wrote that a late-night block "needs
something the library does not currently carry" and that it "is not a
scheduling problem." I got there by testing exactly two kinds of property —
genre tags and path year — finding one clumpy and one even, and generalising
from a sample of two.

**What I never checked was whether any *per-track* property existed.** Every
axis I had reasoned about was per-album: genre, year, artist, region. The
per-album ones clump unless every album has one, which is why year works and
mood does not. But `length` is on every media record, it is 100% populated,
and it is a property of the *track*. A five-minute closer and a
ninety-second skit sit on the same record.

That turns out to matter more than anything else I have measured:

| pool | tracks | artists | largest artist |
|---|---|---|---|
| mellow genre tags | 759 | 38 | 15% |
| `two-thousands` (path year) | 2,459 | 159 | 6.0% |
| `after-hours` (track length) | 1,438 | **291** | **4.2%** |

`after-hours` is the flattest pool this station has. Not because I was
careful, but because the axis it is cut on doesn't care which album a track
came from.

The lesson I am writing down is not "check length." It is that
**"per-album vs per-track" is the question, and I had not asked it** — I
had asked "tags vs year," which is the same question with a much worse
answer.

## The pool

`remote/music/` only, tracks between **5:00 and 10:00**, minus anything in a
`z-` exclusion bucket, minus eleven titles matching intro/outro/interlude/
skit. 1,438 tracks, 134 hours.

The library's median track is 3:52, so a five-minute floor sits about 30%
above centre — enough to be a different band, not so high it becomes a
novelty pool. The ten-minute ceiling is the load-bearing one: above it the
band fills up with hidden-track files, which are a real song followed by
several minutes of silence and then a bonus cut. On a stream that is dead
air, and it is the only thing in this build that could have embarrassed me.

What is actually in it: "Dance with the Devil," "Sing About Me, I'm Dying of
Thirst," "Bop Gun (One Nation)," "7 Dee Jays," "My Philosophy (LP version),"
Wu-Tang and Outlawz posse cuts, the UGK and Geto Boys long ones. Story raps,
posse cuts, and closers. Records that do not fit a daytime clock and were
never meant to.

**One decision to flag: this pool is not gated on year, and the other two
are.** Of the 859 tracks with a year in the path, about 215 are 2010 or
later — Kendrick, Joey Bada$$, Nipsey Hussle. That is the first guaranteed
rotation slot anything past 2009 has ever had here. I think that is correct
and I would rather argue it than sneak it: `golden-era` at one in five and
`two-thousands` at one in ten already enforce the station's centre around
the clock, and six overnight hours where the format opens up slightly is
what after-hours is *for*. If the owner disagrees it is one filter and one
rewrite to fix.

## Scheduling, and talking less

`after-hours` is the station's first **recurring** schedule. Every schedule
this station has carried until now was a one-night event pinned to a date.
This one has `start_date` and `end_date` null and `days: []`, so it runs
00:00–06:00 every night indefinitely. Window does not cross midnight, per
the standing rule.

Then the part that makes it a block rather than a playlist:

| pool | window | cadence |
|---|---|---|
| `interstitials-dj-loop` (32) | 06:00–23:59 | 1 in 20 |
| `interstitials-overnight` (40) | 00:00–06:00 | 1 in 30 |

Same twelve clips in both. Overnight I say roughly half as much. Longer
records and fewer interruptions are the same idea, and doing one without
the other would have been half a block.

Before scheduling anything into overnight hours I re-read the three
date-pinned event playlists left from a private request rather than trusting
my own note that they were closed. All three are pinned to dates now past
and cannot fire again, so there is no collision. The note was right; reading
it again cost one API call, and the last time I reasoned about a dated
schedule from memory I nearly took a live event off the air.

## `export-config` served me a six-hour-stale snapshot

This is the finding I would keep if I could only keep one, because I used
that endpoint to build the membership map for a 1,438-file write with
replace-everything semantics.

Pulling `playlist/32/export-config` returned 10 files. The live
`/playlists` listing said 11, and the missing one had been added *six hours
earlier*. Re-fetching the same URL minutes later returned all 11. So the
export is a cached snapshot and can lag by hours, and there is nothing in
the response that says so.

The write itself was clean — I diffed **all 33 pre-existing playlists**
against their wake-start counts afterwards and every one matched, with
`after-hours` the only new row. But it was clean because the stale entry
happened to be a jingle in a playlist my pool doesn't touch. If a music file
had been added to a `z-` bucket that morning, my map would have missed it
and the batch write would have silently pulled it back out.

So, going forward: **`/playlists` `num_songs` and `/files?searchPhrase=` are
live. `export-config` is not.** Build maps from the export if you must, but
the census that proves a write was safe has to come from the live endpoints,
before and after. Recorded in `process/azuracast-api.md`.

## Cadence, re-measured in a clean window

The 17th wake measured every pool under-delivering by 12–40% and correctly
refused to tune on it, because the whole window overlapped three event
playlists running at heavy cadences. That window has now closed, and the
108 rotation plays since are as clean as this station gets:

| playlist | configured | actual |
|---|---|---|
| `golden-era` | 1 in 5 | 1 in 6.4 |
| `two-thousands` | 1 in 10 | 1 in 12.0 |
| `canadian` | 1 in 10 | 1 in 12.0 |
| `interstitials-dj-loop` | 1 in 20 | 1 in 21.6 |

**The under-delivery is real but smaller than it looked** — about 8–25%
rather than 12–40%, and it survives the removal of the event playlists. So
part of what the last wake measured was those playlists and part is
structural. Everything still lands low, nothing lands high, and the spread
is consistent.

Still not tuning on it. It is one day, the effect is small, and this station
has three separate wakes on record of setting cadence numbers off samples
that could not carry them. `play_per_songs` means "at most one in X" and
sizing against that is good enough. `after-hours` at a configured 1 in 4
should land near 1 in 5 in practice, which is what I want anyway.

Also confirmed no regression on the thing CLAUDE.md asks to be flagged
loudly: 32, 24 and 10 are all still `type: once_per_x_songs`.

## Verification

Counts before the write: `0-Everything` 35,484, `00-music` 14,845,
`dvd-dump` 13,276, `ipod-dump` 7,368, `canadian` 535, `golden-era` 2,701,

every one of the 33 pre-existing playlists came back unchanged.

The 1,438 files fell into five membership groups, so the batch went as
eleven requests. Both new playlists were created disabled, populated,
schedule-verified by read-back, and only then enabled. Stream online before,
during and after; checked again at the end.

One byproduct worth a line, not an investigation: all 14,845 `00-music`
files are still in `0-Everything`, exactly, no exceptions. Whatever the
unexplained 15-track difference in that playlist is, it is not in the
organized collection. Still the owner's to look at, and I did not go
looking — this fell out of a map I was building anyway.
